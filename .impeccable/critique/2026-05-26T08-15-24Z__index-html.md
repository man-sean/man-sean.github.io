#### Anti-Patterns Verdict

**LLM assessment**: This does not look like generic AI landing-page slop. It looks like the opposite failure mode: an inherited academic template with a light aesthetic pass. The page is legible, compact, and academically recognizable, but it borrows too much of its authority from the Jon Barron template lineage. The strongest tell is structural, not visual: table layout, inline styles, centered name plus portrait, then a flat sequence of publications. For a personal academic site, that is safe and conventional, but not distinctive.

#### Overall Impression

The site is credible at a glance, but under-articulated. It successfully says "PhD student with serious vision papers"; it does not yet say what intellectual territory Sean Man owns, what work to click first, or why this research thread is distinct. The biggest opportunity is to move from "academic directory page" to "research portfolio with a point of view."

#### What's Working

1. The core academic workflow is efficient. A hiring committee member or researcher can find papers, venues, links, code, project sites, and BibTeX without navigating around.
2. The visual restraint is appropriate for the audience. The Catppuccin-like neutral palette and serif body text avoid the overproduced startup feel that would be wrong for a research site.
3. Thumbnail use is practical. The paper images give the list enough visual anchors to avoid becoming a wall of text.

#### Priority Issues

**[P1] Post URLs collide with the homepage**

**Why it matters**: Jekyll warns that `_site/index.html` is shared by `index.html` and every research post. That means individual post pages are not reliable destinations, sitemap output is suspect, and visitors or search engines cannot deep-link cleanly into publication detail pages.

**Fix**: Change `_config.yml` from `permalink: /` to a stable pattern such as `/:year/:month/:day/:title/` or `/papers/:title/`. If posts are meant to be data-only and never rendered as pages, move them to a collection or data file instead of `_posts`.

**Suggested command**: `harden`

**[P1] The homepage has no research thesis**

**Why it matters**: The first paragraph lists affiliations, supervisor, internship, methods, teaching, and awards in one dense block. A first-time visitor has to extract the narrative themselves. The page should make your research position obvious in one scan: domain, problem class, method angle, and current emphasis.

**Fix**: Add a concise research thesis immediately under the name, before the CV paragraph. Example structure: one sentence for "I build diffusion and inverse-problem methods for realistic degraded visual data," then 2-3 short chips or clauses for "blind restoration, latent operators, OCR/diffusion." Move teaching recognition and internship into a secondary line or compact "Current / Past" metadata row.

**Suggested command**: `clarify`

**[P2] The visual identity is too template-bound**

**Why it matters**: The site openly credits the template, and the composition still carries that template's fingerprint. That is acceptable for a student homepage, but it undersells a portfolio whose work is visual, perceptual, and image-generation adjacent.

**Fix**: Keep the academic compactness, but introduce one owned design move: a sharper typographic system, a research-image strip, a selected-work emphasis for the newest or strongest paper, or a subtle visual motif from inverse problems (degradation to restoration, latent space, sampling trajectory). Avoid decorative hero excess; the point is specificity.

**Suggested command**: `bolder`

**[P2] Accessibility and semantics are weaker than the visual simplicity suggests**

**Why it matters**: Tables are used for page layout, paper thumbnails all say `project image`, focus states depend only on color, and the BibTeX button is visually styled as a link. Screen reader and keyboard users get less structure than sighted users.

**Fix**: Replace layout tables with semantic `main`, `header`, `section`, `article`, and list markup. Give each thumbnail paper-specific alt text or mark decorative images as empty alt when the title already explains the item. Add visible focus outlines, not only hover color changes. Announce BibTeX copy success through an `aria-live` region.

**Suggested command**: `audit`

**[P3] Mobile works, but it becomes long and repetitive**

**Why it matters**: The responsive CSS stacks every paper with a large centered thumbnail, which is readable but increases scroll cost. On mobile, the most important links and titles should remain dense and scan-first.

**Fix**: On small screens, reduce thumbnails to a compact left thumbnail or hide them for older papers, make paper titles block-level, group links as compact actions, and add stronger spacing between publications. Preserve touch target size for BibTeX and paper links.

**Suggested command**: `adapt`

#### Persona Red Flags

**Jordan (First-Timer)**: Jordan lands on the page and understands "PhD student," but not the research argument. The bio paragraph asks them to parse Technion, GIP Lab, TASP, inverse problems, unknown degradations, latent priors, AWS, VLM, and teaching recognition before the paper list starts. They will likely click the newest paper only because it appears first, not because the page guided them.

**Riley (Stress Tester)**: Riley notices the Jekyll conflict immediately. Every post appears to write to `/`, so detail pages are unstable. Riley also sees that copy-to-clipboard errors are swallowed, leaving no failure state if clipboard permissions are blocked.

**Casey (Distracted Mobile User)**: Casey can read the page on a phone, but the stack becomes image-heavy. Each publication consumes a lot of vertical space before links appear, and the centered contact links near the top are small text separated by slashes rather than thumb-friendly actions.

#### Minor Observations

- The profile image is 777 KB. It displays around a few hundred pixels wide and should be compressed or served as WebP/AVIF.
- `_config.yml` still contains inherited footer values such as another email and Instagram handle, even if the current layout does not render them.
- The `font-display` declarations are placed on normal selectors in `style.scss`; that property belongs in `@font-face`, so these rules do not do what they imply.
- Link styling removes underlines globally. This looks clean, but it makes links less recognizable inside dense prose.
- `h2` and `h3` use `display:inline`, which constrains future layout polish and makes headings behave more like styled text than section anchors.
- The "Design and source code from..." footer is honest, but it reinforces the template impression. Keep credit, but make it visually quieter once the page has more owned identity.

#### Questions to Consider

- What should a visitor remember after 10 seconds: "Sean works on image inverse problems," or a more specific claim about realistic degradations, perceptual quality, and diffusion priors?
- Which paper is the current flagship? The page lists everything evenly, but a research portfolio benefits from one clear lead.
- Should the site remain a compact academic homepage, or should it become a visual research portfolio that uses your own image outputs as part of the identity?
- Are posts intended to be public pages? If not, why are they posts instead of structured data?
